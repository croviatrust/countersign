01 · THE DENIAL

The model card that named nothing

A lab ships a frontier model. The card never names a single training source. A year later, in discovery, counsel argues they "always disclosed responsibly." Who holds the hourly, Bitcoin-anchored record that says otherwise?

02 · THE EDIT

The disclosure that quietly appeared

A training-data paragraph shows up on a card the week a lawsuit is filed. Their git history is theirs to rewrite. Crovia's map isn't — the hour the card first said it, and every hour before when it didn't, are committed under a root that Bitcoin already timestamped.

03 · THE LAW

Article 53 wanted a summary

The EU AI Act requires a public summary of training content. For the models we observed between January and June 2026, that summary was mostly absent — and Crovia can name the exact hours it stayed absent.

The category nobody else is building

FMTI rates what companies choose to disclose. Ranking sites rank what's popular. Both measure presence.

Crovia certifies, with a public randomness beacon on one side and a Bitcoin block on the other, what a company did not disclose — and for exactly which hours. We call the protocol TACET: verifiable silence.

Presence is easy: it's already published. Absence is the hard part — you have to prove a negative, contemporaneously, in a way that survives cross-examination. It's the only part that holds up in court.

The LACUNA certificate

TACET level-2 silence proof · wrapped in a Crovia Seal

A LACUNA certificate states one cryptographically defensible fact: during a defined window, Crovia fetched a model's public surfaces hour by hour, ran the published predicate, and found no training-data disclosure — each observation signed, each hour opened by drand and closed by a Bitcoin anchor. How a silence becomes evidence

every hour · nothing can be backdated
01 · Open

The epoch starts with a drand beacon round nobody can predict. Anything committed under it provably happened after that hour began.

02 · Observe

Crovia fetches each model card over TLS, keeps the bytes, and runs the public predicate. Result, hash of the page, certificate and IP are signed as a snapshot.

03 · Commit

Every snapshot updates one slot in a sparse Merkle map. The new root, the previous sheet and the beacon go into a signed epoch sheet. Slots move forward only.

04 · Anchor

The sheet hash is timestamped on Bitcoin via OpenTimestamps. Now the hour is sandwiched: after the beacon, before the block. A silence proof is the chain of those sandwiches.
